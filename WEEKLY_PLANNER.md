## How to Know When You've Completed a Week

1. **Open the Issue** for your current week
2. **Work through the checklist** - check off each task as you complete it
3. **Submit your deliverable** - usually a pull request or a comment in the Issue
4. **Answer the reflection questions** - write a short summary of what you learned
5. **Wait for mentor review** - Eisa or Don will review your work and give feedback
6. **Get approval** - when your mentor approves, you're ready for the next week

---

## How to Show Your Mentors What You've Done

### Option A: Code Pull Requests
When you write Python code:
1. Commit your changes to a branch
2. Create a Pull Request with a clear description
3. Request review from `@Eisa`
4. Mentors will review and give feedback

### Option B: Issue Comments
For non-code work:
1. Leave a comment in the Issue describing what you did
2. Attach files or links if relevant
3. Mention `@Eisa` or `@ConradLab` to notify them
4. Wait for feedback

### Option C: Reflection Answers
At the end of each week:
1. Answer the reflection questions in the Issue
2. Explain what you learned in your own words
3. Mention any questions or blockers
4. Submit for mentor review

## Research Tracks

### Track 1: Computational Foundations (Weeks 1-3)

You'll learn the tools you need to be a research programmer:

- Week 01: Access your institutional systems
- Week 02: Navigate Linux and connect to Prime8
- Week 03: Write Python code and use Git

**Why it matters:** These skills are essential for all future work.

### Track 2: SATINN Research (Weeks 5-12)

You'll understand and rebuild a published machine learning system:

- Week 05-06: Study SATINN and reproduce it
- Week 07-10: Redesign and implement in Python
- Week 11-12: Explore future research directions

**Why it matters:** This is real research used in the Conrad Lab.

### Track 3: Independent Research (Weeks 3-12)

You'll develop your own scientific research idea:

- Week 03-04: Define your research problem
- Week 05-06: Review the scientific literature
- Week 07-08: Write your hypotheses (Specific Aims)
- Week 09-10: Refine your approach
- Week 11-12: Present your proposal

**Why it matters:** This gives you experience proposing original science.

## What If I Get Stuck?

**For any problem:**

1. **Check the relevant guide** - see [docs/onboarding/](docs/onboarding/) for Linux, Python, Git, etc.
2. **Search existing Issues** - another student might have had the same problem
3. **Create a Blocker Issue** - use the [Blocker template](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/blocker.yml)
4. **Message your mentor** - Eisa and Don respond within 24 hours

## Understanding Issue Status

As your mentor reviews your work:

- **Planned** - Not yet started
- **In Progress** - You're working on it right now
- **Blocked** - You're stuck and need help
- **Awaiting Review** - You've submitted work; waiting for feedback
- **Completed** - Mentor approved; you're ready for next week
